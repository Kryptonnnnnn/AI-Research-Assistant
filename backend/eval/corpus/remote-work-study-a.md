# Remote Work and Productivity: Study A (Software Firm)

This study followed 420 engineers at a mid-sized software firm for eighteen months after the company allowed fully remote work. Productivity was measured by completed work items per engineer per month and by manager-reviewed code quality scores.

## Findings

Output rose after the move to remote work. Completed work items per engineer increased by 13 percent compared with the previous office-based year, and the improvement was largest among engineers with long commutes. Code quality scores did not change significantly. Voluntary attrition fell by about a quarter, which the authors attribute to greater flexibility.

## Interpretation and limits

The authors conclude that fully remote work improves productivity for individual-focused software tasks. They note that the firm already used asynchronous tools and written documentation before the change, and that the results may not generalise to roles that depend on constant face-to-face coordination. New hires who joined during the study reported weaker mentoring relationships than established staff.
