# Remote Work and Productivity: Study B (Customer Support Centre)

This field experiment randomly assigned 310 customer support agents at a large call centre to work fully remotely or in the office for twelve months. Productivity was measured by resolved tickets per hour and by customer satisfaction ratings.

## Findings

Output fell among the remote group. Resolved tickets per hour decreased by 8 percent relative to office workers, and customer satisfaction ratings were slightly lower. Remote agents reported more difficulty getting quick help from colleagues when handling unusual cases. Voluntary attrition did not differ between the two groups.

## Interpretation and limits

The authors conclude that fully remote work reduces productivity for tasks that depend on quick informal coordination and supervision. They caution that the call centre had little experience with remote tools at the start of the experiment and that a hybrid schedule was not tested. The sample consisted mainly of entry-level agents.
