# Coral Reef Ecosystems

Coral reefs are built by colonies of tiny animals called polyps that secrete calcium carbonate skeletons. Although reefs cover less than one percent of the ocean floor, they support roughly a quarter of all marine species, providing shelter, breeding grounds and food for fish and invertebrates.

## Symbiosis

Most reef-building corals live in partnership with microscopic algae called zooxanthellae that reside inside their tissues. The algae perform photosynthesis and supply the coral with sugars, which can account for the majority of the coral's energy needs. In return the coral provides protection and compounds the algae need. This partnership explains why reefs thrive in clear, shallow, sunlit tropical waters that are poor in nutrients.

## Threats

When water temperatures rise just a degree or two above the normal summer maximum, corals expel their algae in a stress response called bleaching, leaving the white skeleton visible. Corals can recover if temperatures fall quickly, but prolonged bleaching causes widespread mortality. Ocean acidification, caused by the ocean absorbing carbon dioxide, lowers the availability of carbonate ions and makes it harder for corals to build skeletons. Other pressures include destructive fishing, coastal pollution and outbreaks of the crown-of-thorns starfish.
